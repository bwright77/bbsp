2014 — 2026 · A legacy Twelve years of work, in one page

Advancing equity in shared micromobility.

The Better Bike Share Partnership launched in 2014 as a one-of-a-kind collaborative between the City of Philadelphia, PeopleForBikes, and NACTO to advance equity in shared micromobility. Specifically, for over a decade, BBSP successfully removed barriers to access and ridership for communities of color, increased diversity in the shared micromobility industry and helped transform the way bike and scooter share programs design and implement their systems.

The legacy of BBSP is not just in its programs, but in the network of leaders and practitioners it strengthened across the country. BBSP created a collaborative community where cities, nonprofits, operators, and advocates could share ideas, learn from one another, and improve how shared mobility systems serve their communities. BBSP also developed and supported a new generation of transportation leaders from a wide range of backgrounds and experiences through initiatives like the Transportation Justice Fellowship.

Where it started

Equity, by design.
Not afterthought.

Bike share took off in U.S. cities around 2008. From the start, BBSP saw who was being left out — and built a partnership to do something about it.

BBSP knew there was a gap in who was being served by bike share when the first systems launched.

The City of Philadelphia knew they wanted to launch a bike share system planned on a foundation of equity — not equity as an afterthought.

The Philadelphia Mayor's Office wanted to make sure their riders looked like the city — a majority-minority city. The partners came together around a common vision: to help bike and scooter share intentionally engage BIPOC and low-income communities in planning and implementation.

Philadelphia used early funding to install 20 stations in underserved neighborhoods, develop a cash payment option, and create a discounted pass program. That blueprint became the BBSP playbook.

How BBSP worked

Four pillars.

Each program reinforced the others. Grants funded critical outreach and education; storytelling spread what worked; peer convenings let practitioners trade ideas, present their work, and learn valuable insights; the Transportation Justice Fellowship seeded the next decade of BIPOC leaders.
PROGRAM · 01

Grantmaking

BBSP implemented a variety of grant programs including Challenge Grants, Emergency Response Grants, Mini Grants, and Living Lab grants. BBSP's grants were invaluable to the organizations and partnerships who received them. The unfortunate reality is that it is challenging to secure funding specifically for outreach and engagement programs. BBSP's grants allowed SMM systems to increase ridership among people of color and in socially vulnerable communities, allowed them to be a more consistent presence that communities could rely on, and once BBSP had invested in these organizations, funding from other sources became easier to secure.

$2.6M+across 125 grants · 59 cities
PROGRAM · 02

Storytelling

Over 500 stories across ten years documented successful equity work in SMM from every angle — a public library of what inclusive SMM looks like in practice. BBSP's storytelling focused on uplifting the voices of those often overlooked in discussions of successful bike share programming and the stories helped seed ideas for more equity projects that were carried out throughout the country. One of BBSP's storytelling goals was to get the word out so that people could replicate the successful outreach and engagement work being done.

500+stories · permanent archive
PROGRAM · 03

Peer-to-Peer Learning Opportunities

Consistent and anticipated peer gatherings and study tours — like the annual Roundtable, an intimate work and play space with 40 people ranging from half-day workshops to multi-day study tours — plus regular peer social functions at national conferences like the National Association of City Transportation Officials' (NACTO) Designing Cities Conference and NABSA's annual conference.

Thirteen Roundtable events over 10 years brought SMM practitioners together for peer exchange. The final 2026 Roundtable drew 40 practitioners from 20 cities to Tucson, AZ for two days of gathering, riding bikes and scooters, and sharing meals. Attendees discussed successes, tackled common hurdles, and brainstormed ways to ensure micromobility truly serves every member of a community. Success stories from BBSP Living Labs grantees and a dialogue between Tucson Vice Mayor Lane Santa Cruz and organizer Vanessa Gallego from Familias Unidas Ganando Accesibilidad (FUGA) reminded us what this work is all about: bike share that truly serves our communities.

13gatherings · 20 cities at Tucson 2026
PROGRAM · 04 · LED BY NACTO

Transportation Justice Fellowship

The fellowship built community and skills among early- and mid-career people of color working to embed mobility justice in transportation. The Fellowship supported and sustained them as they did the heavy work to operationalize equity across the transportation field. Each year, between eight and twelve Fellows were welcomed to the program. They participated in skills-building sessions, collaborated on small projects, and received one-on-one coaching from experts working at the intersections of transportation and social justice. Fellows also helped program NACTO's annual Designing Cities conference.

43 fellows graduated over the life of the program — a career accelerator for BIPOC leaders in transportation justice, building a pipeline of diverse leadership whose impact will outlast the fellowship itself.

Stories from the ground · Living Labs

The big
investments.

Living Labs were BBSP's deepest investments — nine multi-year grants of $200,000 each, across two cohorts and eight cities (Philadelphia twice), that gave cities, transit agencies, nonprofit operators, and community organizations the time to pilot, pivot, and accelerate programs otherwise impossible. A true Living Lab, these projects tested ideas and found what really worked for their communities. We've selected four case studies — two from each cohort — that show what that time and trust made possible on the ground.

Living Lab · 2021–2023

Bikes for Belmont Cragin.

A neighborhood-led plan that turned bike infrastructure into community infrastructure.

The Northwest Center, a nonprofit in Belmont Cragin, used their Living Lab grant to empower youth, build community, and shine a light on transportation needs in their neighborhood. Youth Transit Ambassadors (YTAs) ran workshops, led community rides, successfully advocated for bike lanes and bike share stations, and participated in state capitol visits to inform their electeds of the transportation needs in their neighborhood.

Story 03 / 04 · Pittsburgh, PA
Living Lab · 2024–2026

Advancing Equitable Outreach in Homewood.

POGOH, operated by Bike Share Pittsburgh, launched its Living Lab to address disparities in access to mobility in Homewood.

Through this initiative, the POGOH Living Lab worked alongside residents, schools, and community organizations to understand the real barriers to bike share use, build trust through consistent local engagement, create pathways for new riders — especially youth and families — and expand infrastructure to better serve community needs.

The detail that sticks

POGOH's Living Lab centered on a holistic, community-driven approach — pairing education, events, and partnerships to reduce barriers and build long-term engagement in Homewood. Through direct engagement led by POGOH outreach staff and community ambassadors, new station locations were identified — all within Homewood or a half-mile radius — strengthening connectivity and making the system more accessible for daily use. Expansion planning wasn't just about adding stations, but about placing them where they could have the greatest impact.

As a result, ridership to and from bike share stations in Homewood increased by 277% during the Living Lab grant period.

The coalition · Roundtables + Fellowship

The mission moves forward because the people do.

BBSP's most durable output isn't a program — it's people, connected. Thirteen Roundtables over ten years brought city staff, transit agencies, nonprofit operators, and community organizers into one room: an intimate working space of 40 practitioners trading what worked, what didn't, and what to try next — reinforced by peer gatherings at NACTO's Designing Cities Conference and NABSA's annual conference.

The Transportation Justice Fellowship deepened that bench. 43 early- and mid-career leaders of color — trained, coached, and connected across cohorts — are now embedded throughout the transportation field, operationalizing equity from the inside and programming the field's biggest convenings themselves.

When the final Roundtable convened in Tucson in 2026, it wasn't a farewell — it was a handoff. The funding ends; the coalition doesn't. BBSP's mission carries forward in every city where these practitioners and fellows now lead.

International legacy

Beyond local wins, an international playbook.

Beyond local wins for individual bike and scooter share systems, BBSP's true legacy lies in its reach. With partners like NABSA, BBSP created lasting change in how shared micromobility serves those who need a variety of mobility options most — and how systems are designed across North America.

“BBSP has played a critical role in the fundamental transformation of SMM by helping cities address systemic and institutional barriers to access for low-income and BIPOC communities. Through funding, research, and technical assistance, BBSP has supported cities in testing new ideas, developing strategies, and learning from one another. As a result, the SMM industry has evolved to a place where equity is at the center of system design and operations, not as an afterthought, but as a core principle. That shift is in large part due to BBSP's leadership and sustained investment in this work.” — Waffiyyah Murray · Indego Program Manager · City of Philadelphia
What we built with partners
  • —Contributed to NABSA's Workforce Diversity Toolkit · a best practices guide for more diverse and inclusive workplaces.
  • —$100K+ contributed to DEIB content and to increasing diversity at NABSA's annual conferences and at NACTO Designing Cities conferences

The playbook travels.

In both directions: when Guadalajara's MiBici expanded with eight new stations sited by women riders through a gender-responsive planning guide, BBSP spread the word — putting the work in front of practitioners across North America so the next system could learn from it.

BBSP did more than support systems in their efforts to get more BIPOC and low-income people on shared bikes and scooters. It helped define what equity in shared micromobility can and should look like.

“The partnership's influence on the shared micromobility industry is evident and will live on through the network of practitioners who will continue to apply what they have learned as a result of being a part of the BBSP family. Biking is better in North America because of the Better Bike Share Partnership.” — Tangier Barnes Wright · Deputy Director of Shared Micromobility, PeopleForBikes
