2014 — 2026 · A legacy Twelve years of work, in one page

Advancing equity in shared micromobility.

The Better Bike Share Partnership is a one-of-a-kind collaborative that helped transform the way bike and scooter share programs design and implement their systems. With a focus on grantmaking, peer-to-peer learning opportunities, storytelling, and workforce development, BBSP elevated expectations and outcomes for the shared micromobility industry. From 2014 to 2026, BBSP worked alongside cities, communities, and operators across America to make that transformation real.

Where it started

Equity, by design.
Not afterthought.

Bike share took off in U.S. cities around 2008. From the start, BBSP saw who was being left out — and built a partnership to do something about it.

BBSP knew there was a gap in who was being served by bike share when the first systems took off around 2008 and 2010.

The City of Philadelphia knew they wanted to launch a bike share system planned on a foundation of equity — not equity as an afterthought.

The Philadelphia Mayor's Office wanted to make sure their riders looked like the city — a majority-minority city. The partners came together around a common vision: to help bike and scooter share intentionally engage BIPOC and low-income communities in planning and implementation.

Philadelphia used early funding to install 20 stations in underserved neighborhoods, develop a cash payment option, and create a discounted pass program. That blueprint became the BBSP playbook.

How BBSP worked

Four pillars.

Each program reinforced the others. Grants funded pilots; storytelling spread what worked; the roundtable let cities trade what they were learning; fellows seeded the next decade of leaders.
PROGRAM · 01

Grantmaking

Living Lab grants gave cohort organizations the capacity to implement, deploy, and accelerate programs otherwise impossible. Mini-grants funded smaller, targeted interventions — outreach, education, community rides — often with outsized results.

Story 01 / 03 · Chicago, IL
Living Lab · 2021–2023

Bikes for Belmont Cragin.

A neighborhood-led plan that turned bike infrastructure into community infrastructure.

The Northwest Center, a nonprofit in Belmont Cragin, used their Living Lab grant to empower youth, build community, and shine a light on transportation needs in their neighborhood. Youth Transit Ambassadors (YTAs) ran workshops, community rides, and state capitol visits — then signed up nearly 3,000 community members for Divvy's income-based D4E membership.

“This was my first time joining the Bikes 4 Belmont Cragin group, and what a nice time I had. I brought my two kids ages 11 and 15 and they could not stop talking about how they loved the bike lanes and the food! We connected with some other youth and started a conversation around what protected bike lanes mean to us, I can’t wait to be more involved and to tell others about what is going on in our community.”— Marizol Hernandez · October 2021
The detail that sticks

The Northwest Center staff member who led the Living Lab was so inspired that he went on to found his own youth-based nonprofit dedicated to transportation justice in Chicago. The grant didn't just fund a program — it created a leader.

Story 02 / 03 · Cincinnati, OH
Living Lab · 2021–2024

Red Bike GO.

A discount program that became a third of the system's ridership.

Red Bike used their Living Lab grant to expand access into underserved neighborhoods through service expansion, community ambassador programs, youth engagement, and data infrastructure. Their approach — thoughtful, community-led, data-backed — became a model other systems could replicate.

The detail that sticks

At the 2026 Roundtable, Red Bike's Executive Director Doug McClintock said it plainly: "Your system isn't broken because it's not paying for itself. Shared micromobility is a public service — just like trash pick-up or buses and trains. It's not about profit-maxxing." That framing is now influencing how cities across the country think about bike share funding.

Story 03 / 03 · Honolulu, HI
Mini Grant · $13,500 · 2022

Everybody Rides.

A small grant that gave a small team the runway to reach an entire community — and a statewide invitation followed.

Bike Share Hawaii received $13,500 — one of BBSP's smallest grants — and used it to completely revamp their low-income access program in four months. The results were disproportionate to the investment in the best possible way.

National legacy

Beyond local wins, a national playbook.

Beyond local wins for individual bike and scooter share systems, BBSP's true legacy lies in its national impact. With partners like NABSA, BBSP created lasting change in how shared micromobility serves those who need a variety of mobility options most — and how systems are designed in the United States.

BBSP did more than support systems in their efforts to get more BIPOC and low-income people on shared bikes and scooters. It helped define what equity in shared micromobility can and should look like.

“BBSP has played a critical role in the fundamental transformation of SMM by helping cities address systemic and institutional barriers to access for low-income and BIPOC communities. Through funding, research, and technical assistance, BBSP has supported cities in testing new ideas, developing strategies, and learning from one another. As a result, the SMM industry has evolved to a place where equity is at the center of system design and operations, not as an afterthought, but as a core principle. That shift is in large part due to BBSP's leadership and sustained investment in this work.” — Waffiyyah Murray  ·  Title / affiliation needed
